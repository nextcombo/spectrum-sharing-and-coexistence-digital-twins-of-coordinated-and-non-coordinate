# Spectrum Sharing and Coexistence: Digital Twins of Coordinated and Non-Coordinated Spectrum Sharing

Introduction: The rapid development of wireless technologies such as cellular communication, the Internet of Things (IoT), wireless sensors, and the upcoming 6G network has led to a surge in spectrum demand. With limited spectrum availability, effective allocation and utilization strategies are crucial for maintaining performance and reliability. Spectrum coexistence allows licensed, optically licensed, or unlicensed networks to share spectrum without causing significant interference. However, managing these networks' interference often requires knowledge of cross-network channels, which can be challenging when there is no close coordination between networks sharing the same spectrum.
Preliminary Work: Since cross-network channels are generally unavailable, previous work on spectrum coexistence largely relies on signal measurement-based strategies. For example, Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance (CSMA/CA) methods [1] and [2] only allow unauthorized transmission during the ON periods of the working cycle, while monitoring authorized transmission activities during the OFF periods. However, if the start of the unauthorized ON period overlaps with the authorized transmission, frame collisions may occur, leading to reduced transmission rates for authorized transmitters. A strategy called listen before talk (LBT) [3] allows unauthorized users to first listen during silence periods and only transmit after not detecting any ongoing transmissions, requiring accurate and fast sensing to ensure effective coexistence. Additionally, Transmit Power Control (TPC) methods [3] and [4] adjust transmit power based on the received signal strength from wireless sensors. However, all these methods have the risk of missing out on a large number of transmission opportunities during off/silent periods and under weaker cross-network channels.
Contribution: In this work, we focus on estimating the core challenges of cross-network channels. To address this issue and inspired by recent advances in real-time digital twins [5], we introduce a novel framework for assisted spectrum coexistence based on digital twins, with key contributions:
·We develop a digital twin-based network framework that coordinates base stations, reducing user equipment (UE) computations, and importantly, approximates previously considered inaccessible channels.
· We have introduced and resolved an optimization problem that had not been addressed in the context of spectrum coexistence management, primarily due to unknown cross-network channels.  
· To address the imperfect channel approximations of digital twins, we propose a robust beamforming method that ensures unauthorized networks operate within interference constraints, regardless of the level of channel errors, while simultaneously optimizing their own network performance.  
Simulation results show that our robust beamforming design effectively handles positioning errors for unauthorized users, addressing the challenges of highly mobile users in digital twin network scenarios. Notably, although our focus is on unauthorized and authorized scenarios, our framework and methods can potentially be applied to other spectrum sharing/coexistence scenarios.  
System Model  
In this section, we evaluate the performance of the proposed digital twin-assisted spectrum coexistence framework using the O1 scenario from the DeepMIMO dataset [14]. For this purpose, we assume "BS 4" as the authorized base station operating, and "BS 6" as the unauthorized base station, both located at a height of 6 meters with 8 antennas each. User positions are marked in yellow, spaced 20 centimeters apart, with each position equipped with 4 antennas. In this setup, some users experience obstruction from buildings at the corners of intersections, creating opportunities for increased power transmission by unauthorized users alongside authorized users.
Figure 3 shows the relationship between the level of bounded error models (BEM) and statistical error models (SEM) at unlicensed and licensed base stations, parameterized by β and λ, respectively, and the effective spectral efficiency. Robust designs handle channel errors, while non-robust designs ignore them. Non-robust designs cause high spectral efficiency at unlicensed base stations but perform poorly at licensed base stations due to occasional violations of interference limits with high power. Despite performing suboptimally in the objective function, robust designs outperform LBT and CSAT methods, which produce low efficiency under continuous licensed user activity. Moreover, to address specific approximation errors in digital twins, we evaluate whether our robust design is resilient to user positioning errors. The results indicate that positioning errors greater than 20 cm are consistent with higher BEM coefficients and lower SEM coefficients.
In Figure 4, we investigate the feasibility of different designs. Here, feasibility rate is defined as the percentage of channel realizations where unlicensed user beamforming designs meet interference constraints without overly prioritizing network performance. The results show that for both non-robust scenarios, their respective error models exhibit a decrease in feasibility rate as errors increase. Adding an error coefficient to our solution is crucial to avoid violating interference power constraints, a step that non-robust designs ignore. Due to its conservative nature, worst-case designs always achieve a 100% feasibility rate, while design limits achieve more than 99% feasibility rate. Considering positioning errors, we consistently achieve over 95% positioning rates within a 20 cm positioning radius, with performance improving as the design becomes more robust to higher error levels. Therefore, robust beamforming designs remain effective even for highly mobile users.
In this paper, we propose a novel method for spectrum sharing and coexistence that leverages digital twins to approximate the channels between nodes operating on different technologies, which is often impractical in non-coordinated networks. By utilizing these approximated channels, we maintain spectrum coexistence between authorized and unauthorized networks sharing the same frequency band. While adhering to interference regulations is crucial, our approach allows unauthorized users to transmit simultaneously with authorized users, even at high power levels in certain situations. This capability stems from the additional information provided, recognizing the sensitivity of these approximation errors compared to traditional signal measurement methods. We introduce two powerful beamforming designs to ensure compliance with interference constraints. Furthermore, we demonstrate the reliability of these designs in handling positioning errors in digital twins. These benefits highlight a promising approach for spectrum coexistence in future wireless networks.
